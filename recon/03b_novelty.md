# 03b — Novelty search: chromatin accessibility as an explanation of cross-cell-line Perturb-seq divergence

Search date: 2026-10-11. Methods: about 50 varied web_search queries, web_extract on the key preprints/papers, and PubMed eutils esearch (5 Boolean queries; the query `Perturb-seq AND K562 AND RPE1 AND (HepG2 OR Jurkat)` returned 0 PubMed hits, and the query combining Perturb-seq, chromatin accessibility and cell-type specificity returned only 2 unrelated GWAS-variant papers). For each extracted full text I checked for the keywords ATAC, accessib*, chromatin, DNase, ENCODE, epigen* and enhancer.

**Claim tested:** no prior paper gives a calibrated estimate of how much chromatin accessibility differences (promoter or distal ATAC) explain differences in knockdown response between K562, RPE1, Jurkat and HepG2 (essential-gene CRISPRi Perturb-seq, Replogle 2022 and Nadig 2025), beyond baseline expression, using a conditional-randomization or feature-side permutation test.

## Overall verdict

**NOT PRE-EMPTED (novel, with moderate confidence).** No paper or preprint found estimates how much cell-line differences in chromatin accessibility (promoter or distal ATAC/DNase) explain cross-line divergence in CRISPRi Perturb-seq knockdown responses after conditioning on baseline expression. No such paper exists with or without a calibrated CRT or feature-permutation test.

- The adjacent 2025–2026 work on the same K562/RPE1/HepG2/Jurkat data explains or decomposes divergence using expression, coessentiality or STRING PPI, not chromatin: TRADE; the response decomposition preprint; COMPASS. The decomposition preprint even reports that cell-line-specific components "cannot be recovered from control expression", which leaves room for chromatin as the next explanatory layer.
- Chromatin-informed perturbation work either measures accessibility *changes* caused by knockdowns (Multiome Perturb-seq, Perturb-ATAC) or uses ATAC for prediction within one line (ChromaPert).
- pertTF lists chromatin accessibility as future work.

**Main residual risks:**
- A preprint posted in the last few weeks that is not yet indexed.
- Supplementary analyses in COMPASS, the decomposition preprint or Maffa et al. 2026 that the extracted text (first ~50k characters) did not cover. Recommended: a quick full-PDF grep of those three before claiming novelty.

## Three closest competitors
1. **Perturbation response decomposition** (Molina et al., bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.07.24.740459). Isolates the perturbation × cell-line interaction on the same CRISPRi lines and shows it is not explained by control expression. It does not test chromatin.
2. **COMPASS** (bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.08.03.742643). Same 4 lines plus 2 more; quantifies cross-line conservation of shared and gene-specific components and explains them with STRING. No ATAC.
3. **ChromaPert** (arXiv 2610.09054). The only perturbation-response model that uses control ATAC as context, but it works within K562 across states and is predictive rather than an attribution/test. (TRADE, Nadig 2025, is the data and divergence-metric source and is a near-equal fourth.)
